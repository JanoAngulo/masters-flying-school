Airplane, start here

Private pilot ground and flight training

The first step toward a pilot career. You fly the Cessna 150 or 152 until you solo, then build the hours for your private pilot license (PPL).

Before you start

  • Student Pilot Authority from CAAP
  • Third-class medical certificate
  • NBI clearance

Ground school subjects

  • Air law
  • Aircraft general knowledge
  • Flight performance and planning
  • Human performance
  • Meteorology
  • Navigation
  • Operational procedures
  • Principles of flight
  • Communication

Flight training

Flying begins once CAAP issues your Student Pilot Authority, during or after ground school. You need at least 8 flying hours and a passed pre-solo exam before your instructor releases you for your first solo. The syllabus then covers maneuvers, cross-country flights, simulated emergencies and flying in busy airspace.

Before first solo
8 hours
Total for PPL, at least
40 hours
Split
20 dual, 20 solo

To apply for your license

  1. Restricted Radio Operator Certificate (RROC) from the National Telecommunications Commission
  2. English Language Proficiency certificate, Level 4 or higher
  3. Private pilot ground school certificate and flight time certificate
  4. PPL application form
  5. Pass the CAAP written exam, then a practical test with a CAAP check pilot

Airplane, after your PPL

Commercial pilot ground and flight training

A commercial pilot license (CPL) lets you be paid to fly. That brings responsibility, so the course builds decision-making and crew resource management on top of advanced flying.

Advanced ground school

About 100 hours, covering:

  • Air law
  • Aircraft general knowledge
  • Flight performance and planning
  • Human performance
  • Meteorology
  • Navigation
  • Operational procedures
  • Principles of flight
  • Radiotelephony

Flight requirements

  • A private pilot license
  • At least 110 more flight hours on top of your PPL time
  • At least 150 hours total for the CPL written exam permit
  • A rating on a higher-horsepower aircraft, at least 10 hours per type
  • Simulator training with instrument ground school, plus 20 hours of actual instrument flight

You can add twin-engine training for a multi-engine rating. The license is issued after the CAAP written exam and practical checkride.

Private helicopter pilot ground and flight

A helicopter reaches places nothing else can, which is why it is used for rescue, aerial inspection and sightseeing. You train on the Schweizer 269 and 300 CB.

Before you start

  • Third-class medical certificate

  • Flight performance and planning
  • Human performance
  • Meteorology
  • Navigation
  • Operational procedures
  • Principles of flight
  • Communication

How helicopter flying is different

A helicopter is less stable than an airplane and needs continuous input. You set the angle of the rotor disc with the cyclic, total rotor thrust with the collective, and balance direction with the anti-torque pedals, all while managing engine power, the radio and the weather. Control skills come with practice; command skills, like anticipation and judgement, are built lesson by lesson. Reading before and after each flight makes both come faster.

As with airplanes, you need at least 8 flying hours and a passed pre-solo exam before your first solo. The license requires the RROC, an ELP certificate, ground school and flight time certificates, a PHPL application, and passing the CAAP written exam and practical test.

Ratings for licensed pilots

Instrument rating

Required for commercial pilots

Fly through cloud, low visibility and at night with confidence, looking outside only for takeoff and landing. You do intensive instrument ground school and at least 20 hours in our CAAP-certified simulator, with emergency scenarios and cockpit resource management. Single- and twin-engine simulation is available. Actual instrument flight follows, then the CAAP written and practical exams.

Ask about the instrument rating

Multi-engine rating

Requires at least a private pilot license

Twin-engine aircraft look complicated until you have trained on one. You complete the Equipment Qualification Course (EQC) ground school and at least 10 hours on our Piper Aztec with a rated instructor, then pass the CAAP written and practical tests to add the rating to your certificate.

Flight instructor course

CFI

For commercial pilot license holders

Takes you to the Certified Flight Instructor initial rating, so you can teach new pilots.

CFII

For commercial pilots with an instrument rating

Takes you to the Certified Flight Instrument Instructor rating, so you can teach instrument flying.
